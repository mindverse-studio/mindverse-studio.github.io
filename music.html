Think Music.
From mind to melody.

A research vision for music guided by imagined musical ideas or estimated mental states, with listening feedback shaping what comes next. Explore it through the manual sound lab.

Try the sound sketch ↗Explore the vision ↗

Research vision · Interactive sound preview

A two-way conversation

Imagination gives it direction.
Listening gives it life.

Think Music explores two BCI-guided directions: imagined musical intent as a starting point, and estimated mental states as context for generating and adapting music. The music you hear and your listening response would help guide the next phrase.

How it could work

A composition that
keeps the conversation going.

Five connected stages, from a private musical direction to a phrase worth keeping. Explore the proposed loop.

01 / Imagine

Begin with an inner musical direction.

A rhythm, a contour, a texture, or a feeling. The research goal is to use task-specific BCI features to help express that intention as a starting point for music.

Musical intention→A direction to explore

02 / Interpret

Make a personal starting point.

A proposed personal model combines calibrated BCI features of imagined musical intent or estimated mental state with musical context. Signal quality and uncertainty determine when to adapt and when to ask the listener.

BCI features + context→Composer constraints

03 / Generate

Give the idea a musical form.

A composer offers a candidate phrase: melody, harmony, pulse, and texture. The aim is a coherent musical interpretation that the listener can respond to.

Musical constraints→A candidate phrase

04 / Listen

Let the response become part of the piece.

The listener hears the music. Their subsequent BCI patterns, together with direct feedback, become candidate signals for the next adjustment.

Music heard by the listener→Response + preference

05 / Refine

Keep what resonates. Change what does not.

Adjust a musical dimension, listen again, and carry forward the phrases the listener likes. The destination is a personal composition shaped through interaction.

Listener response→The next version

A proposed research architecture: participant-specific calibration, signal-quality checks, and validation against the listener’s own preferences.

The research direction

Personal music.
Questions worth testing.

Can calibrated neural features contribute a useful musical direction? Can a response to a phrase support a better next version? And does the listener prefer the result?

Our vision connects musical imagery, conditional composition, and listener feedback. Published work provides context; the full Think Music BCI loop remains a research direction to develop and evaluate.
