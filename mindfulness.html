Five faculties.
Mind at the centre.

Ego
The place-and-time perspective of “I, here, now.” Notice when an experience becomes “my thought,” “my concern,” or “my decision.”
Memory
Knowledge and past experience available to Mind. Notice which associations, expectations, or familiar stories appear.
Intellect
Strategy and reasoning. Notice how you compare possibilities, interpret a situation, and consider a next step.
Sense
Observation. Notice sounds, sights, bodily sensations, and the changing experience of the present moment.
Action
Orchestration from intention into a response. Notice the urge to act, the choice you make, and what follows.

A conceptual map for reflection. The animated signals illustrate connections between the faculties; Ego traces a decision history through time.

A brief mindfulness practice

Watch a choice
take shape.

Take a minute or two with a small, everyday choice: responding to a message, changing a song, or reaching for your phone.

Feel where your body meets the chair or floor. Let your breathing stay natural. Observe with curiosity, without needing to judge each thought or change it.

Move between these prompts as they become relevant. When attention wanders, notice that too, then gently return to a sound, a sensation, or your breathing.

  1. Sense · What is here?

    Notice the sound, sensation, or event that draws your attention. Describe what you experience before adding a story.

  2. Memory · What comes to mind?

    Notice a familiar association or expectation. Silently acknowledge “remembering” or “expecting,” and see what happens next.

  3. Ego · Where does “I” appear?

    Notice a personal interpretation: “I need to answer,” or “What will they think of me?” Observe it as a thought arising in this moment.

  4. Intellect · What choices appear?

    Notice how the mind weighs a response. Is there room to reply, wait, ask a question, or leave the matter for later?

  5. Action · What do you choose?

    Pause briefly. Choose a response, then notice the action and its immediate effect. Return your attention to the present moment.

An everyday example: a message arrives +

You hear a notification. A previous conversation comes to mind. The thought “I should reply immediately” appears, followed by a few possible answers. You notice the urge, take a pause, and decide whether to respond now or later.

The practice is to notice that unfolding: sensation, association, personal meaning, deliberation, and response. You can return to the same observation during music, movement, or a quiet moment of meditation.

Reflection Space

Notice a moment.
Put it into words.

What drew your attention today? What did you remember, how did you interpret it, and what did you choose to do? Write a few lines with curiosity.

Other starting points: a small moment of gratitude, an emotion you noticed, or an intention for tomorrow.
