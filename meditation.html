Guided Meditation.
A practice that listens.

A research vision for meditation guided by BCI recordings, estimated mental states, and your own feedback. Explore layers of practice, with guidance that could adapt its pace and focus to the person.

Explore the layersTry a quiet practice

BCI-informed guidance

Notice the state.
Guide the next moment.

The proposed experience combines incoming BCI recordings with a personally calibrated model of neural dynamics. Estimated states and their uncertainty would help suggest when to remain with an anchor, soften a prompt, or move to another layer of practice.

Your own experience stays central: you can keep a practice, change it, pause, or return to a familiar anchor. Self-reported attention and comfort would help evaluate whether a suggestion is useful.

The website currently offers written practices, an optional breathing cue, and an artistic chakra gallery. Live BCI tracking and adaptive guidance remain a research vision.

Layers of practice

Begin where you are.
Choose your own depth.

These layers describe ways of practising. Move between them at your own pace, guided by attention, comfort, and personal feedback.

01 Arrive

Notice contact with the chair, floor, or bed and the sounds around you. In a BCI-enabled session, this would also provide time to check recording quality and establish personal context.

02 Settle

Choose a comfortable anchor: natural breathing, a sound, or a visual focus. A future guide could adjust the amount and timing of its prompts using your feedback and calibrated state estimates.

03 Sustain

Stay with the chosen anchor and notice attention changing. Guidance could invite a gentle return when the person finds it useful, while tracking uncertainty in the estimated state.

04 Explore

Try a broader field of awareness, a reflective theme, or a symbolic visual focus. Choose how much guidance you want and return to an earlier layer whenever you wish.

05 Integrate

Notice how the session felt and choose a small intention to carry into the next activity. Your reflection could help personalize future guidance.

Guided Meditation / A quiet practice

Look gently.

Use the artwork as a simple focus for contemplation. Choose a centre, notice its form, and give yourself a moment before moving on.

Three small invitations

  1. 01 / Arrive

    Choose one image

    Sit comfortably or simply pause where you are. Let your breathing remain natural. Choose a centre and notice its colour, edges, and open spaces.

  2. 02 / Attend

    Follow a form or word

    Trace a petal with your gaze or silently recall the displayed seed syllable. If sound is part of your own practice, approach it in the way you have learned.

    Notice the ordinary

    Let the image go. Notice your surroundings, name one thought from the reflection, and return to your day at your own pace.

Guided Meditation / Symbols & tradition

Many traditions.
One considered interpretation.

The chakra is a wheel or lotus in the symbolic language of yoga and tantra. This gallery presents a contemporary artistic interpretation of a familiar root-to-crown sequence.

How to read this gallery

Meet the symbol
on its own terms.

Lotus petals, geometric centres, elements, and seed syllables form the visual vocabulary. These are spiritual and contemplative correspondences; the gallery presents them as symbols rather than anatomical diagrams or medical claims.

The red-to-violet palette is a contemporary convention. Historical texts and living lineages use different colours, names, locations, and sound associations. Our artworks combine the familiar palette with traditional lotus motifs, while keeping the interpretation visible.

We retain Muladhar, Swadhisthana, Bishuddhi, and Agna in the gallery. The centre guide also includes common Sanskrit spellings such as Mūlādhāra, Svādhiṣṭhāna, Viśuddha, and Ājñā.

The seed sounds

A syllable.
A point of attention.

Lang, Vang, Rang, Yang, and Hang are shown alongside Laṃ, Vaṃ, Raṃ, Yaṃ, and Haṃ, often written Lam, Vam, Ram, Yam, and Ham. The final mark indicates nasal resonance; spelling and pronunciation differ across teaching traditions.

Om is used here for Agna. Sahasrara is presented with silence and with Om as a lineage-dependent association, rather than assigning it another gross element or one universal seed sound.

The small reflection prompts are original invitations to observe and reflect. The website is a visual gallery, with no recorded mantra playback or automated assessment of a meditative state.

Guided Rest

Settle into
a quieter moment.

Short written practices to follow at your own pace. Choose one, pause when you wish, and let the instructions remain gentle.

A simple body scan

Notice where your body meets a chair, floor, or bed. Bring attention gently to your feet, legs, hands, shoulders, and face. Notice sensations without needing to change them. Rest attention wherever it feels comfortable.

Release a little effort

Notice whether you are holding an unnecessary effort in your hands, jaw, or shoulders. If comfortable, let a little of that effort soften. Leave your breathing natural, and return to the support beneath you.

An evening wind-down

Let one small task be finished for today. Notice the room around you and the sounds that remain. You may choose a story, a field recording, or a quiet pause before continuing your evening.
